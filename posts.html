The 2026 Total Solar Eclipse in Spain

Published: August 2026

On August 12, 2026, Spain will experience one of the most exciting astronomical events of the century: a total solar eclipse. During a total solar eclipse, the Moon passes directly between the Earth and the Sun, completely blocking the Sun's light for a few minutes.

The path of totality crosses northern Spain, meaning that cities within this path will briefly experience daytime turning into night. Stars and planets may become visible, temperatures can drop slightly, and the Sun's beautiful outer atmosphere, called the corona, becomes visible to the naked eye.

Outside the path of totality, much of Spain will still see a deep partial solar eclipse, with a large portion of the Sun covered by the Moon.

How to Observe It Safely

Never look directly at the Sun without certified eclipse glasses that meet the ISO 12312-2 safety standard. Ordinary sunglasses are not safe. Only during the brief period of totality—if you are inside the path of totality—is it safe to remove eclipse glasses. As soon as the Sun begins to reappear, put them back on.
